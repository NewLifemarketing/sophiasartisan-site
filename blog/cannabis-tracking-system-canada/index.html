The cannabis tracking system Canada uses, and what licence holders report

By the 15th of every month, each federally licensed site accounts to Health Canada for every unit and kilogram of cannabis it held. This is what the law requires, what the report contains, and why it exists.

By the 15th of every month, each federally licensed processing site in the country tells Health Canada how much cannabis it started the previous month with, how much came in and why, how much left and why, and how much remained at the end. The cannabis tracking system Canada built under the Cannabis Act is, at its core, that reconciliation, repeated for every licensed site, every month, with no month skipped. A site with nothing to report still files.

What the system is for

Part 6 of the Cannabis Act gives the reasons in one section. Section 81 allows the Minister to establish and maintain a national cannabis tracking system to enable the tracking of cannabis, to prevent cannabis from being diverted to an illicit market or activity, and to prevent illicit cannabis from being a source of supply in the legal market. Section 82 lets the Minister order classes of licensed persons to provide information about their activities with cannabis, and the order has to specify what information, by when, and in what form.

The section also draws a line. An order under it may not require personal information about a consumer who purchases cannabis at the retail level. The data that feeds the cannabis tracking system Canada operates is about inventory and movement between licensed and authorized businesses, not about the person at the counter. Section 83 then sets out who the Minister may share it with, including provincial governments and bodies for the purpose of verifying compliance with provincial law.

What a licence holder reports

The order made under section 82 is the Cannabis Tracking System Order. Section 2 applies to holders of several federal licence classes, processing among them, and requires the following for each licensed site, for the previous month:

  • opening inventory on the first day of the month, counted as a number of cannabis products and, separately, as a quantity of unpackaged cannabis
  • additions during the month, broken down by reason: received from another licence holder, packaged and labelled for retail sale on site, returned, or any other reason
  • reductions during the month, again by reason: sold or distributed, destroyed, lost or stolen in circumstances that require notice to the Minister, returned, or any other reason
  • sales to provincially authorized sellers, by the province where the buyer is located
  • closing inventory on the last day of the month, as a number of products, a quantity of cannabis and a book value

Every figure is reported by class. The Order's schedule separates edible cannabis that is in solid form at a temperature of 22 ± 2 °C from edible cannabis that is not, and both are measured in kilograms. A moulded chocolate is a solid edible. The distinction between a cannabis product and unpackaged cannabis matters as well: the Order adopts the Regulations' definition of a cannabis product, which is cannabis after it has been packaged and labelled for sale to a consumer at the retail level, so a batch of chocolate before packaging and the finished packages it becomes are counted in different parts of the same report.

Health Canada's monthly reporting guide is direct about the deadline and the exceptions there are not: reports are due no later than the 15th, and a licence holder must submit one even in a month with no sales or no inventory.

The CTLS, and what it holds

The Order requires the information to go through a dedicated website established by the Minister. That is the Cannabis Tracking and Licensing System, or CTLS. Health Canada's user guide describes it as a secure web portal in which a licence holder can submit new licence applications, manage current licences and report on licensed activities. Monthly reports can be entered by hand for one site at a time, or uploaded as a file, and the licence holder may choose either method each month.

The submission is not the whole obligation. Section 7 of the Order requires the records behind the information to be kept for at least two years from the day it is provided, because a monthly figure means something only if it can be traced back to what produced it.

Why the numbers have to reconcile

A tracking report is a balance sheet in units and kilograms. Opening inventory plus additions, minus reductions, has to equal closing inventory, and each line has to agree with something else the licence holder is already required to record. A reduction for loss or theft points to a separate obligation: under section 246 of the Cannabis Regulations, a theft or a loss that cannot be explained by normally accepted business activities must be reported to a police force within 24 hours and to the Minister in writing within 10 days. Production figures point back to the lot records that section 225 requires for every lot of edible cannabis.

That is the practical reason inventory discipline starts at the receiving door. Our cannabinoid inputs and food ingredients are received under the record-keeping the licence requires, and every lot moves from formulation to a quality assurance person's release through the documented sequence set out on our craft manufacturing page. The monthly report summarises those records. It does not replace them.

What may change

On 26 September 2026, the Canada Gazette, Part I published a proposed Order Amending the Cannabis Tracking System Order (Streamlining of Requirements). It would remove book value reporting and some site information, consolidate the 14 classes of cannabis to seven, and end reporting under the Order by provincial bodies and by provincially authorized distributors and retailers. The proposal records 846 federal licence holders subject to reporting as of 31 December 2025, and it opened a 45-day period for comment. It is a proposal, not law in force. Until a final order is made, the cannabis tracking system Canada's licence holders report into is the one described above.
