Standardized cannabis symbol

The standardized cannabis symbol: where it goes and what it signals

One small red mark is required on almost every legal THC product in Canada. The Cannabis Regulations say exactly when it applies, where it sits, how small it can be, and what may not imitate it.

The standardized cannabis symbol is not a brand asset and not a design choice. It is a fixed graphic owned by the Government of Canada, and the Cannabis Regulations define it by reference to a document Health Canada publishes. A licence holder does not draw it, adapt it or recolour it. They obtain it from the Minister as an electronic file and place it where the regulations say it goes.

That makes it one of the few elements on a legal package that is identical across every brand. Everything about it is specified: when it is required, where it sits, how small it may be, what surrounds it and what may not resemble it.

When the standardized cannabis symbol is required

The trigger is a concentration, not a product class. Under section 123(1)(f) of the Cannabis Regulations, the label on any container of a cannabis product that contains THC in a concentration greater than 10 μg/g must carry it. The concentration is assessed taking into account the potential to convert THCA into THC, so a product cannot fall under the threshold simply because some of its THC is still in acid form.

Ten micrograms per gram is 0.001% by weight, a very low bar. Some arithmetic shows how low. One milligram of THC is 1,000 micrograms, so 1 mg spread through 100 g of product sits exactly at 10 μg/g. Any product carrying a declared milligram quantity of THC in a package weighing less than that is over the line.

Health Canada's packaging and labelling guide adds a point the regulations do not state directly. A licence holder may use the symbol voluntarily on a product at or below 10 μg/g. The guide suggests doing so for large-format products, such as a beverage, where the concentration is under the threshold but the total quantity of THC in the container is not trivial.

Where it must appear, and how large

On the label

Section 130(5) sets five requirements for the symbol on a label:

  • It must appear in the upper left 25% of the principal display panel. Where a package has separate English and French principal display panels, it appears in the upper left 25% of each.
  • It must be at least 1.27 cm by 1.27 cm.
  • It must have a white border of at least 2 points on all sides.
  • Its text must read left to right when the container is displayed under the customary conditions of purchase and use.
  • If it is resized, its dimensions must stay proportional vertically and horizontally.

The regulations borrow the meaning of principal display panel from the Consumer Packaging and Labelling Regulations. Fixing the symbol to the upper left of that panel puts it in the same region of the front of every legal product. Health Canada's consumer guide to reading a label puts it plainly: the symbol is required on the front of the product.

On a wrapper

A product can also be packed in a wrapper, a layer in direct contact with the cannabis inside its container. Section 123.1 bars any brand element from a wrapper and allows only images or information that a law or regulation authorizes. The standardized cannabis symbol is one of them, and in some cases it is mandatory. Where the cannabis in direct contact with the wrapper exceeds 10 μg/g THC, the symbol must be clearly and prominently displayed on the wrapper's exterior surface, with the same 1.27 cm minimum, the same 2-point white border and the same rule on proportional resizing. The upper-left placement rule is specific to the label.

Its colour and its surroundings

Health Canada's page for the standardized cannabis symbol sets out its colours exactly: a red specified as PANTONE 185 (C0 M100 Y92 K0), with black and white. The packaging rules then work around it. Section 113 requires the colour of a container's surfaces to contrast with the red of the symbol and with the yellow of the health warning. The type rules that govern other required text, such as black sans serif lettering on white, do not apply to the symbol itself; its appearance is fixed by the published file.

What the symbol signals, and what it does not

Health Canada's guide describes the purpose in one line: the symbol is used to inform and warn consumers that the cannabis product contains THC. It is a presence marker. It says that THC is in the product above a set concentration, and it says it the same way on every package.

It does not say how much. The quantity is a separate requirement. On an edible in discrete units, section 132.18 requires the label to state the THC each unit could yield, preceded by "Total THC per unit", and the THC the whole package could yield, preceded by "Total THC". A product at the 10 mg package limit and a product far below it carry the same symbol. Reading the number is a separate step, covered in what 10mg THC means on a legal Canadian edible.

Two further rules protect what the symbol signals. Section 131 prohibits any illustration, sign, mark, symbol or design on a container that so closely resembles the standardized cannabis symbol that it is likely to be mistaken for it. And section 130(9) caps the size of a brand: where the symbol is required, an image used as a brand element may be no larger in surface area than the symbol itself. On a legal package, the brand mark is never bigger than the warning that THC is inside.

This is part of why the front of a legal package looks so constrained. The symbol, the health warning and the cannabinoid statement claim their space first, and a single permitted brand element fits around them. The wider set of rules is covered in why legal cannabis packaging looks the way it does.

For anyone reading a package, the standardized cannabis symbol answers exactly one question, whether the product contains THC above 10 μg/g, and it answers it in the same position and the same red on every legal product in the country. Everything else on the label answers the questions that follow.

FAQ

When does a cannabis product have to carry the red symbol?

Under section 123(1)(f) of the Cannabis Regulations, when it contains THC in a concentration greater than 10 μg/g. That is 0.001% by weight, a very low bar.

Where on the package does the symbol go?

In the upper left 25% of the principal display panel, and of each one where English and French panels are separate. It must be at least 1.27 cm by 1.27 cm with a white border of at least 2 points.

Does the symbol show how much THC a product contains?

No. It marks that THC is present above the threshold, the same way on every package. The quantity is a separate label requirement, stated after Total THC per unit and Total THC on an edible in discrete units.

Can a brand use a design that looks like the symbol?

No. Section 131 prohibits any mark or design so closely resembling the symbol that it is likely to be mistaken for it. Where the symbol is required, an image brand element may be no larger in surface area than the symbol itself.
